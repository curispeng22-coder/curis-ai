## 2. Exploration chronology

### Stage 1 — Early A/B/C product-screen themes

Frames: `3:6`–`3:5118`.

- A tested neutral restraint, compact density, and limited warm accents.
- B tested cool blue-gray surfaces, readable dark layering, and softer separation.
- C tested stronger executive contrast and green-led focus.
- Product Composition: `FAILED` across these screens.
- Visual Language: A and B remain `USEFUL`; C's contrast principle remains useful but its black/green palette is `SUPERSEDED`.

### Stage 2 — Pure Visual Language Research

Frames: `13:3`–`13:6`.

The exploration correctly separated abstract visual language from Curis page composition:

- A — Precision / Quiet
- B — Clear / Human
- C — Distinct / Executive

Each board contains Light/Dark foundation, surface stack, typography, accent, semantic color, and material study. Composition is `NOT RELEVANT`; visual value is `STRONG`.

### Stage 3 — Fused Candidate V1

Frames: `23:3`, `23:203`, `23:403`, `23:668`, `33:2`.

The declared fusion was:

- Foundation from A;
- comfort/material from B;
- brand tension from C.

The real-screen stress test introduced a Mineral Blue branch. The pages preserved useful dark hierarchy and surface evidence, but the page composition did not pass and Mineral Blue was later explicitly rejected as a fixed brand direction.

### Stage 4 — Warm Company branch

Frames: `42:3`, `42:203`, `42:403`, `42:668`, `49:2`.

The branch tested whether warmth could make Curis feel more human/company-like. It produced open composition and employee-attribution evidence, but the visual logic drifted into terracotta, warm canvas, and brown-black dark surfaces. CEO feedback explicitly rejected warm yellow/beige, terracotta, warm brown canvas, brown-black dark, and “warm equals earthy” logic. Visual status: `SUPERSEDED`.

### Stage 5 — Neutral Foundation + Brand Energy

Frames: `52:3`–`52:6`.

This was the decisive reset from “theme skinning” to a stable neutral foundation with separate brand energy:

- Light: bright neutral white / soft gray without blue, yellow, beige, or dirty-white bias.
- Dark: neutral graphite, dimensional and comfortable, without blue-black or brown-black tint.
- Five energy families were compared on the same foundation: coral-red, iris/violet, teal, indigo, red-orange.

The foundation remains `STRONG`. The comparison did not freeze a winner.

### Stage 6 — Iris Accent validation

Frames: `61:2`, `61:3`.

The study narrowed iris to exact inputs `#806BE5`, `#6778E0`, and `#5E3096`, preserving each original color in Light and Dark. It tested mark, action, selection/focus, employee identity, company rhythm, result attribution, and semantic collision. `#9A89F0` appeared only as an optional dark tone alongside the unchanged `#806BE5` baseline.

The board itself selected no winner. Subsequent CEO briefs named `#806BE5` the Primary Brand Accent Candidate and `#9A89F0` the Dark Accent Candidate for the next validation stages. That is evidence of a chosen candidate for iteration, not a Frozen visual standard.

### Stage 7 — Company Presence

Frames: `68:3`, `68:203`, `68:403`.

The Operating Rhythm semantic model became legible: `Result → Owner → Current Work → Next Responsibility`. Quiet State and employee continuity also passed semantically. The pages still looked too much like an AI Workspace/Productivity Tool, so Product Composition remained `PARTIAL`.

### Stage 8 — Company Spatial Identity

Frames: `75:3`, `75:160`.

The work removed the hero-card mental model, reduced container count, and shifted the sidebar toward company/workspace identity. CEO feedback recorded that these frames already looked substantially less like SaaS Card UI, but the main area was too empty, the operating scene too dispersed, and navigation still menu-like. Product Composition: `PARTIAL`; Visual Language: `STRONG`.

### Stage 9 — Final Spatial Polish

Frames: `81:3`, `81:113`.

These frames introduced calmer density, tighter operating-scene grouping, subtle spatial fields/dividers, stronger company identity, and sparse iris focus. They are the best representatives of the latest visual/spatial language, but the page composition was not finally accepted. Product Composition remains `PARTIAL`; Visual Language remains `STRONG`.

### Stage 10 — V1 closure attempt

The later V1 closure attempt did not generate or modify the required source frames because the requested accepted source set was not present. It therefore adds no new Figma visual direction and must not be mistaken for a later design iteration.

## 3. Visual direction clusters

### Direction A — Precision / Quiet

Core feeling: exact, neutral, calm, durable.

Color: neutral whites and charcoal; low-chroma supporting colors; accent used sparingly.

Hierarchy: typography and disciplined contrast lead; metadata recedes without disappearing.

Surface language: precise borders, restrained luminance steps, low elevation.

Navigation: compact and typographic; selected state uses fine focus/border treatment.

Density: compact-to-balanced.

Strength: strongest analytical clarity and long-session neutrality.

Risk: can become austere, mechanical, developer-adjacent, or spatially empty.

Best representatives:

1. [Board A — Precision / Quiet · `13:3`](https://www.figma.com/design/19almI2sZYcrkIW5JV6KTb/Curis-Theme-Exploration-Review%E3%80%82?node-id=13-3)
2. [Neutral Foundation — Light · `52:3`](https://www.figma.com/design/19almI2sZYcrkIW5JV6KTb/Curis-Theme-Exploration-Review%E3%80%82?node-id=52-3)
3. [Neutral Foundation — Dark · `52:4`](https://www.figma.com/design/19almI2sZYcrkIW5JV6KTb/Curis-Theme-Exploration-Review%E3%80%82?node-id=52-4)

### Direction B — Clear / Human

Core feeling: clear, comfortable, approachable, editorial.

Color: airy cool-neutral Light; readable charcoal Dark; accents kept gentle.

Hierarchy: softer contrast and generous spacing carry warmth without beige.

Surface language: luminance-led separation, lightly tactile surfaces, limited borders.

Navigation: calm and legible, with low-pressure selected states.

Density: balanced-to-open.

Strength: best long-session comfort and human readability.

Risk: can drift toward a document/productivity application or generic Enterprise SaaS when blue-gray dominates.

Best representatives:

1. [Board B — Clear / Human · `13:4`](https://www.figma.com/design/19almI2sZYcrkIW5JV6KTb/Curis-Theme-Exploration-Review%E3%80%82?node-id=13-4)

3. [Operating Result — Dark · `23:203`](https://www.figma.com/design/19almI2sZYcrkIW5JV6KTb/Curis-Theme-Exploration-Review%E3%80%82?node-id=23-203)

### Direction C — Distinct / Executive

Core feeling: confident, decisive, higher-tension, executive.

Color: stronger neutral contrast with vermilion/mineral/copper experiments; dark surfaces stay mineral rather than luxury-black.

Hierarchy: decisive typography and scarce accent make the primary focus unmistakable.

Surface language: open areas with sharper material relationships; border and accent are deliberate.

Navigation: stronger positional hierarchy and focus marker.

Density: balanced, with high contrast between primary and supporting content.

Strength: strongest brand and operating-product potential among the abstract boards.

Risk: can feel severe, luxury-black, financial, or over-directed if contrast/accent expands.

Best representatives:

1. [Board C — Distinct / Executive · `13:5`](https://www.figma.com/design/19almI2sZYcrkIW5JV6KTb/Curis-Theme-Exploration-Review%E3%80%82?node-id=13-5)

### Direction D — Neutral Foundation + Fresh Iris Company Space

Core feeling: bright, modern, calm, company-like, with controlled youthful energy.

Color: accent-free Light/Graphite foundation plus sparse iris; `#806BE5` is an explored candidate, not a frozen standard.

Hierarchy: A-like precision, B-like material comfort, and C-like primary-focus tension.

Surface language: open canvas, broad tonal fields, dividers, and spatial proximity before containers.

Navigation: company identity and workspace grouping replace the strongest feature-menu rhythm; selected state uses a small iris cue.

Density: calm density—substantial quiet space with one coherent operating scene.

Strength: best evidence that visual language can express “workspace itself is the company,” employee presence, and responsibility continuity without card walls.

Risk: page composition is still partial; empty-space balance, navigation-as-directory, and evidence/iris semantic proximity remain unresolved.

Best representatives:

## 4. Color and theme evolution

### `#32f08c`

- Position in yesterday's new exploration: a read-only exact-paint audit across all 40 inventoried frames found zero `#32F08C` occurrences.
- The early C frames did test different greens (`#185C36`, `#28A060`), but that is not the same color and the black/green logic was later rejected.
- `#32f08c` belongs to the legacy implementation/Frozen-spec lineage. For this retrospective it is `LEGACY IMPLEMENTATION VISUAL BASELINE`, not an automatically preferred visual standard.

### `#806BE5`

- First explicit appearance in this chronology: Iris Accent Validation `61:2`, after the five-family Brand Energy board narrowed the search from general iris/violet.
- Why it appeared: the prior green, Mineral Blue, terracotta, and warm/brown directions had been rejected; Curis needed fresh, contemporary energy on a stable neutral foundation without AI-gradient cliché.
- Verified exact use across all inventoried frames:
  - `61:2`: 25 solid-paint occurrences plus explicit original-hex labels.
  - `61:3`: 15 occurrences.
  - `68:3`: 13 occurrences.
  - `68:203`: 13 occurrences.
  - `68:403`: 24 occurrences.
  - `75:3`: 9 occurrences.
  - `75:160`: 11 occurrences.
  - `81:3`: 9 occurrences.
  - `81:113`: 11 occurrences.
- CEO feedback status: explicitly kept as the Primary Brand Accent Candidate for Company Presence/Spatial validation, with `#9A89F0` as a future Dark candidate. No token or Frozen visual standard was authorized.
- Current retrospective status: surviving candidate evidence, not current standard and not an automatic winner.

### Other explored accents

- Early A: cobalt, teal, plum; some orange/brown dark residue.
- Early B / Fused Candidate: ocean/cool blue and Mineral Blue (`#4F6FAF` Light, `#7E9ED8` Dark).
- Early C: deep greens plus vermilion/mineral/copper studies.
- Warm branch: terracotta/copper (`#B7654F`, `#D77B63`)—explicitly rejected.
- Brand Energy: coral-red `#D65359`, iris `#775BC7`, teal `#248C91`, indigo `#5266D8`, red-orange `#E05E3C`.
- Iris validation: `#806BE5`, `#6778E0`, `#5E3096`; optional Dark tone `#9A89F0` shown without replacing the original baseline.

### Light / Dark maturity

- Abstract maturity: A/B/C boards and Neutral Foundation cover both Light and Dark comprehensively.
- Real-screen maturity: Candidate V1 and Warm Company branches cover Operating Result and Employee Workstation in both themes.
- Accent validation maturity: Iris candidates were compared in both themes and against semantic colors.
- Company-space maturity: Company Presence, Spatial Identity, and Final Polish were Light-only by design. Dark company-space application remains unvalidated.
